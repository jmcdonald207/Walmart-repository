# Walmart Investment Analysis

## Executive Summary

Walmart is a high-quality, highly scaled retailer with improving operating margins, strong customer traffic, and emerging higher-margin businesses in advertising, memberships, marketplace services, and fulfillment. Fiscal 2025 performance was strong relative to the prior two years, but capital expenditures increased and free cash flow declined from fiscal 2024.

The current recommendation is **watch—defer**. Walmart's operating performance supports continued research, but the analysis does not yet include the historical share price, market valuation, expected return, or a completed downside case needed to recommend purchasing the stock.

## Initial Thesis

Walmart merits further research because fiscal 2025 results show substantial scale, positive comparable-sales growth, and continued growth in its core U.S. operation. Walmart U.S. comparable sales increased 4.8%, driven by transactions and unit volumes, while eCommerce contributed approximately 2.9 percentage points to comparable-sales growth. This suggests growth was supported by customer activity rather than price alone.

The conclusion is **watch-defer**, not initiate. The 10-K establishes operating momentum and financial capacity, but it does not establish that Walmart's shares offered an adequate expected return on the valuation date. A strong company can be an unattractive investment when its market price embeds overly optimistic expectations.

## How Walmart Makes Money

Walmart earns most of its revenue by buying merchandise from suppliers and reselling it through Walmart stores, Sam's Club locations, websites, mobile applications, pickup, and delivery. The economic model is high volume and relatively low margin: Walmart uses its purchasing scale, inventory turnover, logistics network, and everyday-low-price positioning to sell large quantities while earning a modest profit on each sales dollar.

The business has three reportable segments:

- **Walmart U.S.** generated $462.415 billion of fiscal 2025 net sales, or approximately 69% of consolidated net sales. It sells groceries, general merchandise, and health and wellness products through stores and eCommerce. Grocery was the largest category at $276.003 billion, approximately 60% of Walmart U.S. net sales. This recurring, needs-based demand drives traffic, although grocery generally carries lower margins than general merchandise.
- **Walmart International** generated $121.885 billion of net sales through operations including Mexico and Central America, Canada, China, India-focused eCommerce activities, and other markets. It adds geographic growth and diversification but also introduces currency, political, regulatory, and execution risk.
- **Sam's Club U.S.** generated $90.238 billion of net sales. It sells merchandise in bulk and also earns recurring membership fees. Membership income is particularly valuable because it is not tied directly to the cost of merchandise sold and is a significant contributor to segment profit.

Walmart also earns revenue or income from several smaller but strategically important activities:

- **Memberships:** Sam's Club memberships and Walmart+ produced $3.8 billion of membership fee revenue in fiscal 2025, up from $3.1 billion in fiscal 2024 and $2.6 billion in fiscal 2023.
- **Advertising:** Walmart Connect and related international advertising businesses sell access to Walmart's digital and in-store audiences. Advertising is strategically important because management identifies it as a higher-margin business.
- **Marketplace and fulfillment:** Third-party sellers pay for marketplace access, fulfillment, and related services, broadening Walmart's assortment without requiring Walmart to own all inventory.
- **Financial and other services:** Walmart earns service revenue from selected payments, money transfer, protection-plan, health, data, and other offerings, subject to significant regulation.

The investment significance is that merchandise retail supplies Walmart's scale and customer traffic, while memberships, advertising, marketplace, and services have the potential to raise profit faster than sales if their higher-margin contribution grows.

## What Is Good and Bad About Walmart

### Strengths — What Is Good

- **Exceptional scale and purchasing power:** Fiscal 2025 consolidated revenue was $680.985 billion. Scale supports supplier bargaining power, broad assortment, low unit costs, and large technology and logistics investments.
- **A defensible value proposition:** Price leadership is useful in both strong and weak economies. Inflation or pressure on household budgets can encourage customers to trade down to Walmart, while improved convenience can attract higher-income households as well.
- **A dense omnichannel network:** Stores are not only retail locations; they also act as pickup points, local fulfillment nodes, return centers, and delivery origins. This can shorten the final mile and makes the physical estate more valuable in an eCommerce market.
- **Recurring and resilient demand:** Grocery, consumables, pharmacy, and health and wellness represent frequent, needs-based purchases. These categories reduce dependence on discretionary spending.
- **Multiple growth engines:** eCommerce, advertising, marketplace, fulfillment, Walmart+, Sam's Club memberships, automation, and international businesses provide paths to grow beyond mature U.S. store retailing.
- **Strong liquidity and access to capital:** Fiscal 2025 operating cash flow was $36.443 billion, and management stated that operating cash flow, cash, borrowings, and capital-market access should be sufficient for anticipated obligations and investment.

### Weaknesses — What Is Bad

- **Structurally thin margins:** Fiscal 2025 operating income was $29.348 billion, only 4.4% of net sales. Small changes in wages, freight, shrink, product mix, markdowns, or pricing can therefore have a meaningful effect on profit.
- **Large capital requirements:** Capital expenditures rose to $23.783 billion in fiscal 2025 from $20.606 billion in fiscal 2024 and $16.857 billion in fiscal 2023. These investments may strengthen the moat, but they reduced fiscal 2025 free cash flow and must earn adequate returns.
- **U.S. concentration:** Walmart U.S. and Sam's Club U.S. produced approximately 82% of consolidated net sales. This limits geographic diversification and makes U.S. consumer, labor, tax, and trade conditions especially important.
- **Low-margin sales mix:** Grocery is Walmart U.S.'s largest category. It creates traffic and resilience, but a shift toward grocery and away from general merchandise can pressure gross margin.
- **Operational complexity:** More than two million associates, thousands of locations, large inventories, international subsidiaries, third-party sellers, digital systems, and regulated services create many possible points of failure.
- **A strong company may still be an expensive stock:** The business quality does not by itself establish an attractive expected return. The missing valuation work remains essential to the investment decision.

## Competitive Advantages

Walmart's principal advantage is a reinforcing scale system rather than any single product. High sales volume supports purchasing power and distribution efficiency; low costs support low prices; low prices and broad assortment attract traffic; traffic makes memberships, advertising, marketplace, and fulfillment more valuable; and those revenue streams can fund further investments in price, technology, automation, and convenience.

Specific advantages include:

1. **Purchasing power and supplier relationships** that are difficult for smaller competitors to replicate.
2. **A large logistics and store network** located close to much of the U.S. population, supporting rapid pickup and delivery.
3. **Customer trust in low prices** built over decades and reinforced by frequent grocery purchases.
4. **First-party transaction data** that can improve inventory forecasting, personalization, advertising measurement, and supplier decisions.
5. **Financial capacity** to invest through economic cycles and spread technology costs over a very large revenue base.
6. **An ecosystem with several monetization methods,** including retail margin, memberships, advertising, seller services, fulfillment, and financial services.

The moat is not absolute. Amazon has greater digital depth in several areas, Costco has a highly productive membership model, and specialized or discount competitors can outperform Walmart within individual categories. Walmart must keep converting scale into lower costs and better convenience for the advantage to remain durable.

## Challenges and Risks

- **Competition and changing customer expectations:** Walmart competes with Amazon, Costco, Target, grocery chains, dollar stores, pharmacies, specialty retailers, digital marketplaces, delivery platforms, and international competitors. Failure to match selection, speed, usability, or price could cost market share.
- **Margin execution:** Wage investments, variable compensation, marketing, depreciation, eCommerce fulfillment, shrink, freight, and unfavorable product mix can offset higher-margin revenue growth. Because the consolidated margin is thin, execution errors can have an outsized earnings effect.
- **Tariffs and sourcing:** A significant portion of U.S. general merchandise is manufactured outside the United States. New tariffs or trade restrictions could increase costs, force price increases, weaken demand, or compress margin.
- **Labor:** Walmart must recruit, retain, train, and manage a very large workforce. Wage inflation, benefits expense, labor shortages, worker-classification disputes, and changes in employment law could raise costs.
- **Technology and cybersecurity:** Omnichannel growth increases dependence on reliable platforms, customer data, AI, automation, and third-party systems. Outages, failed technology investments, fraud, or a material data breach could disrupt operations and damage trust.
- **Regulatory and legal exposure:** Walmart faces extensive rules covering antitrust, product and food safety, pharmacy operations, consumer finance, payments, employment, privacy, tax, environmental practices, and international investment. The 10-K identifies active matters involving Flipkart in India, competition issues in Mexico, and the Spark driver platform.
- **International execution and currency:** Foreign operations expose Walmart to exchange rates, local competition, ownership restrictions, political conditions, and different consumer behavior. Reported U.S.-dollar results can weaken even when local-currency operations improve.
- **Consumer and macroeconomic conditions:** Weak discretionary demand can hurt general merchandise, while inflation can raise labor and product costs. Deflation can also reduce reported sales dollars even if unit demand remains sound.
- **Capital-allocation risk:** Automation, fulfillment, remodels, acquisitions, and new services require substantial capital. Returns may disappoint or take longer than expected, while excessive repurchases at a high share price could destroy value.
- **Reputation and stakeholder pressure:** Product quality, labor practices, supplier conduct, health services, data use, and environmental issues can produce litigation, regulation, boycotts, or customer distrust.

> **Central thesis risk:** Higher-margin activities may fail to offset the cost of omnichannel growth and the margin pressure inherent in grocery-heavy retail. Persistent sales growth without corresponding operating-income and free-cash-flow growth would weaken the investment case.

Overall, fiscal 2025 was stronger than Walmart's recent history in revenue, operating income, operating margin, net income, eCommerce scale, and membership revenue. Walmart U.S. comparable sales rose 4.8%, with eCommerce contributing about 2.9 percentage points, and management attributed the increase to transactions and unit volumes rather than price alone. That is favorable evidence about demand quality.

The caution is cash conversion. Free cash flow did not match the improvement in accounting profit because Walmart accelerated capital investment. The key historical question is therefore not whether Walmart has grown, but whether current spending will produce enough durable margin improvement and incremental cash flow to justify its cost.

## Outlook

As of the March 14, 2025 valuation date, Walmart's operating outlook appears favorable, but the stock outlook remains unresolved without a market valuation.

### Base Case

The base case is continued moderate sales growth, supported by grocery and health and wellness demand, market-share gains, eCommerce, and convenience. Operating income could grow somewhat faster than sales if advertising, marketplace, fulfillment, and membership revenue become a larger part of the mix and automation produces expense leverage. The physical network should remain useful because it supports both store traffic and local fulfillment.

### Bull Case

Walmart turns its scale and customer traffic into a meaningfully higher-margin ecosystem. Faster advertising and membership growth, sustained eCommerce profitability improvement, successful automation, international growth, and continued share gains across income groups lift returns on capital and free cash flow.

### Bear Case

Mature retail growth slows while wages, depreciation, fulfillment, tariffs, shrink, and technology spending remain elevated. Higher-margin businesses remain too small to change consolidated economics, capital expenditures stay high, and free cash flow disappoints. A premium stock valuation would amplify the downside if results merely normalize rather than deteriorate.

## Research Plan

1. **Reconstruct historical performance.** Extract at least three years of consolidated and segment revenue, operating income, margins, diluted EPS, operating cash flow, capital expenditures, and share counts from the 10-K.
2. **Evaluate earnings quality.** Reconcile net income to operating cash flow and free cash flow; identify working-capital effects, unusual items, disposals, and noncash charges.
3. **Underwrite operating drivers.** Analyze comparable sales, transactions, average ticket, eCommerce contribution, membership income, unit counts, retail square footage, inventory, and segment margins.
4. **Normalize capital intensity.** Separate estimated maintenance capital expenditure from growth investment and evaluate returns on incremental invested capital.
5. **Refine enterprise value.** Review debt footnotes, leases, restricted or operational cash, noncontrolling interests, pensions, and other claims.
6. **Establish the market baseline.** Obtain Walmart's March 14, 2025 closing share price and calculate equity and enterprise value using internally consistent dates and share counts.
7. **Value the company.** Apply multiple-based and intrinsic-value approaches using normalized earnings and free cash flow.
8. **Build scenarios.** Create bull, base, and bear cases for sales growth, comparable sales, margins, reinvestment, free cash flow, and exit valuation.
9. **Define decision thresholds.** State the required return, margin of safety, sizing constraints, and facts that would trigger initiation, continued deferral, or rejection.
10. **Prepare the committee case.** Present the thesis, variant perception, valuation, catalysts, risks, thesis breakers, and decision-relevant unknowns.
